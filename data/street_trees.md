# Street Trees and Urban Forestry — City of Maplewood

> **FICTIONAL DEMO POLICY.** This document was written for a software demo. It is not official guidance from any real government. "City of Maplewood" is a made-up city.

## Who owns street trees
Trees in the strip between the sidewalk and the street (the "tree lawn") are **owned and maintained by the city**. Residents may not prune, remove, or attach anything to a street tree without a **Tree Work Permit**, which is **free**.

## Free tree planting
Residents can request a **free street tree** through the **Plant-a-Maple** program. Requests are accepted from **January 1 to March 15**, and trees are planted each **October**. Residents may choose from **eight species**, including red maple, swamp white oak, and ginkgo (male trees only).

## Damage and removal
Report a damaged, dead, or dangerous street tree by calling 311. Emergency hazards are addressed within **4 hours**. Routine pruning requests are completed within **90 days**.

## Penalties
Damaging or removing a street tree without a permit carries a fine of **$500 per tree**, plus the cost of a replacement tree.

## Heritage trees
Trees on private property with a trunk diameter of **36 inches** or more are designated **Heritage Trees**. Removing one requires approval from the Tree Board, which meets on the **second Wednesday** of each month.

## Unusual rules
- Every street tree is given an official **name** when planted. Residents may look up their tree's name by calling 311.
- Tree hugs on city property are limited to **30 seconds per hug** during the spring pollen season (April 1 to May 31) so others may have a turn.
